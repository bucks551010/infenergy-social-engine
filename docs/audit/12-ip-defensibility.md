# Document 12 - Intellectual Property and Defensibility Review

## Scope and caution

This is an engineering/business issue-spotting review, not a patentability opinion or legal advice. Novelty, non-obviousness, freedom to operate, inventorship, ownership, and prior art require qualified counsel and a professional search. Classical persuasion methods, generic agent orchestration, prompts, and ordinary workflow automation may be difficult to patent broadly.

## Protection portfolio

- **Copyright:** source code, schemas as creative expression, documentation, visual assets, training material. Register key releases where economically justified.
- **Trade secrets:** selection weights, evaluation thresholds, implementation playbooks, customer-specific learned patterns, unpublished benchmarks. Require access controls, confidentiality, repository discipline, and exit procedures.
- **Trademarks:** company/platform/category names after clearance; protect the service source identifier, not technical function.
- **Patents/provisionals:** consider only after counsel identifies a specific technical mechanism with plausible novelty and commercial blocking value. File before public disclosure where applicable.
- **Contracts:** define background IP, customer data/content, configured deliverables, improvements, feedback, model/provider rights, contractor assignment, and residual knowledge.

## Real moat versus aspirational moat

**Real now:** working code and tests; operational integration experience; detailed conversion/governance libraries; Infenergy-specific product/creative knowledge.  
**Weak now:** prompts, agent names, model access, generic scheduling, and broad “AI social automation” claims.  
**Build next:** measured outcome corpus, evaluation benchmark, vertical policy packs, reliable integrations, certified delivery process, multi-tenant control plane, and customer trust from auditable operation.

## IP hygiene actions

1. Execute invention and copyright assignments with every contributor/contractor.
2. Preserve dated architecture records, commit history, test evidence, and inventor contributions.
3. Inventory third-party libraries, model terms, training/reference image licenses, fonts, and customer assets.
4. Remove sensitive runtime data and secrets from public repositories and patent disclosures.
5. Create disclosure review before demos, publications, and open-source releases.
6. Commission trademark clearance and a targeted prior-art landscape.
7. Decide intentionally what remains secret versus published for defensive credibility.

## Counsel questions

Who invented each candidate and under what agreement? Was any mechanism publicly disclosed or sold? Which claims produce a technical effect beyond business rules? Are reference images and generated assets commercially licensed? Do provider terms affect ownership or training use? Can customer learning improve the platform without violating confidentiality? What open-source obligations attach to distribution or hosted use?
